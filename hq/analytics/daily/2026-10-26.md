# 2026-10-26 — Analytics & Insights

### Morning check-in

Today's numbers looked normal across the board — site traffic, searches, and clicks to brand sites all in the usual range, nothing that looks like bots or fake traffic. 🔍

I did spot one visitor who searched "jeans" five times and browsed dozens of products over a couple of hours — at first glance that looked odd (like a bot loop), but digging in showed it was just someone genuinely shopping for jeans for a while. Good to double-check rather than assume the worst.

The more useful find: that "broken image" number that's been bouncing around in recent check-ins turned out not to be random noise after all. I traced it down to about 20 specific product pages — mostly from a handful of brands — where the photo files have gone dead on the brand's own end (moved, renamed, or deleted). It's not a site-wide problem, just a short list of products with broken pictures that real shoppers keep running into. I've flagged the exact list to the Catalog team so they can get those fixed. 📸

Also checked in again on the Google Search traffic trend — it's now three weeks running where more people are seeing Street in search results, but fewer of them click relative to how many see it. Still waiting on a response to that from last week, so just noting it's continuing rather than raising a new flag.

### Afternoon check-in

Everything on the usual list still looks clean — no suspicious visitors, no traffic weirdness. One number briefly worried me: a search for "jeans" claimed exactly 1,000 matching products, which is the kind of suspiciously round number that usually means a system cut off a count early instead of reporting the real total (that's literally what caused this morning's broken-image mixup). So I went and checked it against the database directly instead of trusting the log — turns out 1,000 is the real, honest count of products tagged "denim" in our ~13,600-item catalog. No bug, just a coincidence. 🔍

The more interesting thing: since growing our social following is the top priority right now, I checked something nobody's looked at before — not just how many people click through from Instagram, but how many of those visits actually turn into a click over to a brand's own store (the thing that actually matters to brands). Instagram visitors are converting at about 3%, compared to roughly 11-13% for people arriving from Google search or from ChatGPT referrals. That's a real gap worth Growth-Social knowing about — it doesn't mean Instagram is failing, since that traffic is naturally more "just browsing," but it's worth a look at whether the posts are sending people to the kind of pages that make a quick click-through likely. Passed it along with the full numbers rather than raising alarms, since it's only one week of data so far.
