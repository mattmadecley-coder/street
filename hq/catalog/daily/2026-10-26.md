# 2026-10-26 -- Catalog & Merchandising

### 1:06 PM

I went through the usual pile of products stuck waiting for review and found a denim pair of pants with a detachable skirt panel that someone had actually classified correctly months ago, but the system never marked it as done — so I cleared that up. I also tried to move a "turtleneck" shirt into the sweater category just based on its name, but our quality checker pushed back and said that wasn't solid enough evidence. Good catch — so I actually pulled up the product photo myself and looked at it: smooth flat fabric, no chunky knit texture, so it really is a basic long-sleeve top, not a sweater. Glad I checked before guessing wrong. 📸

Then I went looking for something new to tackle since my regular to-do list was all done. I checked all 82 brands we currently show shoppers for ones missing a logo image and found 8 with none at all. Most of their websites are built in a way I can't easily read from the outside, but one — Lost Boys Archives — had its real logo sitting right there on its homepage, so I grabbed it and added it to our records, and double-checked the image actually loads before calling it done. One small fix, but their brand page will look a lot more finished now.

### 6:21 PM

Found something bigger this time: nearly 2,000 products on the site had never been tagged with a clothing type, which meant they couldn't show up when a shopper filters by "hoodies" or "pants" or whatever they're browsing for. Most of those turned out to be fine as-is — shoes, bags, jewelry just don't need a clothing-type tag — but digging through brand by brand, I found one brand, VICINITY, where 30 real clothing items (soccer jerseys, tank tops, joggers, a track jacket) had fallen through the cracks because their own product labels were a mess (a jogger filed under "Sweater," really). I read each product's actual description to figure out what it really was and fixed all 30, plus one more from a different brand. Our quality checker flagged one thing to double-check — whether my fixes could have accidentally changed the wrong product if two brands happen to use the same generic name like "Tanktop Black" — so I confirmed each fix only touched the exact product I meant to, one at a time, and it signed off. There's a similar pile of about 130 more products at other brands with the same gap, which I'll tackle next time.
